Chapter 7 · Interior Painting

The technique that makes jobs look professional.

Interior painting is where most painters spend the majority of their time. Done right, it looks effortless. Done wrong, every flaw is visible every time the light hits the wall.

The professional sequence — always follow this order

  1. Protect floors, furniture, and fixtures
  2. Remove outlet and switch plate covers
  3. Complete all surface prep (patch, sand, caulk, prime)
  4. Paint the ceiling first
  5. Paint the walls (cut in first, then roll)
  6. Paint the trim and doors last
💡 Always paint top to bottom, trim last. Drips from the ceiling get covered when you do walls. Drips from walls get covered when you do trim.

Cutting in — the skill that defines your quality

  • Load your angled brush — about 1/3 of the bristles into the paint, no more
  • Start about 1/2" from the edge and work toward it, letting the bristle tip do the work
  • Use a steady, smooth stroke — don't rush
  • Keep a wet edge — if your cut line dries before you roll, you'll see a visible line
  • Practice on cardboard before your first job

Rolling technique

  • Load roller fully — roll out excess on the tray grid until evenly saturated
  • Apply in a top-to-bottom motion — don't change direction on the same wall
  • Maintain consistent pressure — don't press harder in some areas
  • Keep a wet edge — always roll back into the section you just painted before it dries
  • Finish with light vertical strokes to eliminate roller marks

Common problems and fixes

ProblemCauseFix
Brush marksOverworking paint or poor brushQuality brush, maintain wet edge, don't overbrush
Roller marksWrong nap or dry rollerMatch nap to surface, keep roller loaded
Drips and runsToo much paintReduce load, catch and brush out immediately
Lap marksEdges drying before overlapWork faster, maintain wet edge
Flashing / uneven sheenUnprimed patches or inconsistent pressureAlways prime patches, consistent pressure
