Chapter 10 · Estimating & Business

The math that makes you profitable.

Most painters who work for themselves fail not because they can't paint — but because they can't estimate. They underbid jobs, run out of money, and burn out. Learning to estimate properly is what separates a painter with a job from a painter with a business.

Measuring square footage

  • Wall area: Width × height of each wall, subtract doors (20 sq ft each) and windows (15 sq ft each)
  • Ceiling area: Length × width of the room
  • Trim: Measure linear feet of baseboard, door casings, crown molding, and window trim
  • Rule of thumb: A gallon of latex paint covers 350–400 sq ft per coat on a smooth surface

Estimating labor

  • Prep takes as long as — often longer than — the actual painting
  • A 12' × 15' room (one coat) takes an experienced painter roughly 2–3 hours setup to cleanup
  • Always add time for: protecting floors, moving items, cutting in, two coats, and cleanup
  • Track your time on every job — your estimates get more accurate with each project

The path from employee to contractor

  • Contractor's license: Most states require one for jobs over a certain dollar amount — check your state's licensing board
  • Business license: Register as an LLC (typically $50–$200) for liability protection
  • Insurance: General liability ($1M minimum) — about $100–$200/month for a solo operator
  • Estimating software: Quick Bid, Jobber, or a well-designed spreadsheet
  • Marketing: Google Business Profile (free), Nextdoor (free), and before/after photos on Instagram
💡 The smart path: Get hired. Work for 2–3 years. Learn the trade AND how a painting business runs from the inside. Save money. Take your first clients on weekends. When your side income matches your salary — make the jump. That's how most successful painting contractors got there.
