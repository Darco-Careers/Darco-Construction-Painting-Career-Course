Chapter 5 · Surface Preparation

Where pros separate themselves.

"Bad prep = peeling paint, callbacks, angry clients. Surface preparation is 70% of the job."

The quality of a paint job is determined before a drop of paint is ever applied. Professional painters obsess over prep. Beginners rush past it. That difference is visible in the finished work — and it's the difference between a one-time job and a referral business.

Cleaning surfaces

  • Remove all dirt, grease, and grime — paint will not adhere to contaminated surfaces
  • Use TSP (trisodium phosphate) for kitchen walls and high-grease areas
  • Use mildicide cleaner on any surface with mold or mildew — never paint over mold
  • Allow surfaces to dry completely before any further prep or painting
  • On exterior surfaces, pressure washing is standard — control the pressure to avoid damage

Repairing damage

  • Small holes (under 1/2"): Fill with lightweight spackle, allow to dry, sand smooth
  • Medium holes (1/2"–3"): Use a mesh patch kit, apply joint compound in thin coats, sand between coats
  • Large holes: Cut out damaged section, install a backing board, patch with drywall
  • Cracks: Widen slightly, fill with flexible caulk or joint compound
  • Woodwork gaps: Always caulk the joint between trim and wall — this is the clean professional line clients expect

Priming — the step beginners skip

  • New drywall: Always prime — raw drywall absorbs paint unevenly (called "flashing")
  • Repairs and patches: Always prime — patches absorb differently than surrounding wall
  • Color changes (dark to light): Use tinted primer to reduce coats needed
  • Stains (water, smoke, nicotine): Use stain-blocking primer (Zinsser BIN or Kilz) — regular primer will not block these
  • Bare wood: Always prime exterior wood before painting
⚠️ Skipping primer on stains is the most common beginner mistake. Apply two coats of regular paint over a water stain and it will bleed back through both coats. Use Zinsser BIN and it disappears permanently.
