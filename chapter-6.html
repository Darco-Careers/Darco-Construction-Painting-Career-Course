Chapter 6 · Paint & Materials

Choosing the right product for every job.

Walk into any paint store and you'll face hundreds of products. Knowing how to choose quickly and correctly separates professional painters from amateurs — and employers will test you on this immediately.

Latex vs. Oil-Based paint

PropertyLatex (Water-Based)Oil-Based
CleanupWater and soapMineral spirits / solvents
Dry time1–2 hours between coats8–24 hours between coats
DurabilityGood — improves with acrylicExcellent — very hard finish
FumesLow VOCHigh VOC — strong ventilation required
Best useWalls, ceilings, most interior workTrim, cabinets, doors, high-wear surfaces

Paint finishes — when to use each

⬜
Flat / Matte
Hides imperfections best. Use on ceilings and low-traffic walls. Not washable.
🥚
Eggshell
Slight sheen. Standard finish for most interior walls. Easy to wipe clean.
✨
Satin
Smooth, moderate sheen. More washable. Good for hallways, kitchens, kids' rooms.
💡
Semi-Gloss
Shiny, highly washable. Standard for trim, doors, and bathrooms.
🪞
Gloss
High shine, hardest finish. Best for furniture, cabinets, and exterior trim.
ℹ️ Quick rule: Higher sheen = more durable and washable, but shows surface imperfections more. Lower sheen = hides imperfections better, but marks more easily.

Specialty products you'll encounter

  • Stain-blocking primer (Zinsser BIN, Kilz): Blocks water stains, smoke, and nicotine permanently
  • Masonry paint: Breathable and flexible — required for concrete, brick, and stucco
  • Epoxy floor coating: For garage floors and industrial surfaces — two-part system
  • Elastomeric paint: Bridges hairline cracks in stucco and masonry
  • Waterborne alkyd: Oil-like finish with latex cleanup — increasingly standard for trim
